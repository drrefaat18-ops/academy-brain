# T02 — Assessment design for micro:bit intro-unit lessons

Merged union of 3 lanes, no adjudication. Applies to L1-s1, s3, s4, s6.

## hermes

- L1-s1, s3, s4, s6 dropped the upstream formal assessment/self-assessment instrument — "assessment" is recorded missing in all 4. — MID:L1-s4
- L1-s1 (name badge) is specifically missing assessment, answer_key, AND differentiation. — L1-s1 — MID:L1-s1
- The official Think/Create/Evaluate/Extend/Share arc gives a built-in Evaluate moment fillable with a brief demo or verbal check instead of a written test. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/
- A non-written exit-ticket question ("point to the block that makes the LED scroll your name") costs under two minutes, fits the closing Evaluate slot. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/
- Demo-based checks — flash the program, show the defined behaviour — turn the running device into direct evidence of understanding. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/
- A 2-minute peer-share ("tell a partner one block you used and why") aligns with the upstream Share phase and surfaces misconceptions. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — MID:L1-s3
- Because each session already consumes the full T/C/E/E/S arc, formative assessment must be embedded as demo/exit-ticket/peer-share, not appended as a separate written instrument. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — MID:L1-s4

## codex

- 60-second device show-and-tell exit check: child runs the name badge, says which block made the output appear. — L1-s1 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/
- Pairs demonstrate one input-output interaction + spoken peer prompt ("What did you press or shake, and what did the micro:bit do?"). — L1-s3 — https://mbit.io/tutorial-emotion-badge
- Gallery walk: learner shows step counter, resets it, explains why the variable starts at a known value. — L1-s4 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/step-counter/
- One-question oral exit ticket: "Which block changes the number after a movement?" — accept pointing/demonstrating as alternatives to writing. — L1-s4 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/step-counter/
- Two-person demo check for rock-paper-scissors: one shakes, other predicts outcomes and describes the program's choice logic. — L1-s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/rock-paper-scissors/
- Use brief Think/Create/Evaluate/Share moments to collect spoken explanations and visible device behaviour during the build rather than a separate written quiz. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/

## opencode

- Single-question exit ticket (oral or point-at-blocks) on what the program does, fits last 5 min of session. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/
- Demo-based checks — pairs hold up their micro:bit running the program — most authentic lightweight assessment, avoids written work. — L1-s3,s4,s6 — https://makecode.microbit.org/
- Peer-share prompts (turn-and-talk) use peer instruction to surface misconceptions about event/loop blocks without grading. — L1-s4,s6 — MID:peer-instruction-cs
- Prediction prompt before flashing ("what will the LEDs show when I press button A?") checks mental-model building. — L1-s1,s3 — https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/
- For a first session, formative assessment should be observational/binary (did it run? can they explain it?) rather than scored. — L1-s1,s3,s4,s6 — MID:formative-assessment-lightweight-kids
- Share-circle where each child shows one thing they made — motivation + teacher coverage check, effective as capstone for L1-s6. — L1-s6 — https://microbit.org/teach/
